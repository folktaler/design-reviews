The system, not the pages

Everything below is a token or a component. There is no page here on purpose: the page types are the next piece of work and they will be built out of these parts rather than alongside them. Every number on this sheet was measured on Airbnb at a real 390 pixel phone viewport, or measured in Chrome on the typeface itself, and the two places it departs from what was measured are marked where they occur rather than in a footnote.

Type — one family, seven combinations

Airbnb renders four hundred and sixty-three text elements in a single family and ships twenty-four size and weight combinations. Seven of those cover four hundred and eighteen elements, so the tail is cut. One person publishing cannot carry twenty-four, and nobody reading in the sun would notice if they could. What is kept is the discipline rather than the count.

The one decision here that is ours and not Airbnb's

Every surface that prints a clock time, a fare, a date or a count switches on tabular figures. Neither Airbnb nor the live site does this. The reason is not tidiness: with the default figures a one is roughly two thirds the width of a zero, so a column of departure times drifts sideways as you read down it, and a drifting column reads as an estimate rather than a fact.

Read the pair above as a demonstration of the FIGURES, not of the clock format. A reader-facing time on this site is written 10am – 1pm, 2 – 4.30pm — lowercase, no minutes on a round hour, a full stop rather than a colon. The 24-hour form survives in the registry, which is where these numerals came from, and in the fares, which are the case tabular figures still wins outright: ₹110 over ₹40 has to line up whatever the clock does. What the format change costs is honest and worth knowing — “10am – 1pm” and “2 – 4.30pm” are not the same width, so a stack of opening hours down a listing page no longer forms the clean column that a stack of “21:00 / 11:00” does. Tabular figures stop the numerals jittering between rows and stay switched on; they can no longer make an hours column square.

Colour — the discipline is how little there is

On an Airbnb search page showing thirty-six listings with prices, badges and ratings, the only red on the page is the logo. Prices are ink. Badges are ink. Nothing is coloured in order to sell it. Amber here has one job, and the job is not selling: it marks the thing the reader came for, which on a page about a place is the date we last went and looked.

Two text greys, not three. Airbnb ships a third at two point one seven to one, used sixty times on one page, and it fails every standard there is. Our quieter ink is seven point four to one, which is forty per cent better than the grey they use for everything secondary. If something needs to recede further than that, it needs fewer words rather than paler ones.

The rule that governs all five is about photographs, and it corrects the study that produced it. A photograph running to the edge of the page is square. A photograph inside a card takes the card’s corner. Walking every picture on the Airbnb listing page found the hero at zero, a photograph inside a card at eight and an avatar at a half, all three resolving the same declaration with a value set per instance. Of the twelve rules that touch it, not one sits inside a media query, so the corner belongs to the component’s job rather than to the width of the screen.

The parts

Buttons — rank is carried by shape

A pill is the one thing to do on this page. A twelve pixel corner is everything else. Both are the same height, so the only difference a reader sees at a glance is the corner, and a corner survives colour blindness, glare and a screenshot forwarded on WhatsApp. Airbnb distinguishes their two ranks exactly this way and it is the single most transferable thing they do.

The facts row

Three rows, three states, and the middle one is the whole business. A fact we have is printed. A fact we do not have keeps its heading and puts the reason where the value would be, because a blank is bad and a named absence is trust. A fact that disagrees with the sign on the door gets both halves and a date, because knowing the posted hours are wrong is the thing nobody else has.

The refusal

A named absence in a facts row is for a fact this record does not carry. This is different: it is for a fact this site has never gone and checked at all — almost always the hours — and it replaces the whole facts row rather than filling one line of it. Primary ink, body size, one plain sentence. Not grey, not italic, not a warning triangle, because a confession set like a disclaimer reads as a lawyer wrote it, and a confession set like a sentence reads as a person did. It renders on 76 of 151 live place pages today.

The trust card

Airbnb’s version of this holds a star rating, a badge and a review count. We have no crowd and cannot manufacture one, so what is taken is the shelf and not the goods: three cells, thin rules between them, a number above its label, and no colour anywhere in it. What sits on the shelf is a record of our own work rather than anyone’s opinion.

The photograph — and its named absence

A photograph that runs to the edge of the page is square-cornered, no border, full width: the hero, above the name. When there isn’t one — true of every one of the 151 live place records today — the absence is one sentence at the foot of the fold, set at the same ink and the same body size as the facts above it, not a grey box holding the hero’s height. Naming what is missing is the rule; giving it the hero was never the rule.

No photograph of this place yet — every photograph of it you would find elsewhere was taken by somebody else, and we do not publish those.

The card

What kind of place, and where. Then the name, in grey. Then the facts. Airbnb puts the proper noun third and demotes it, because a reader looking at a list is deciding rather than recognising, and this is the row order a travel index has needed all along. The live site does the opposite.

The sticky action bar

Shown here in the flow of the page rather than pinned, because a bar fixed to the bottom of a specimen sheet covers the specimen. Airbnb’s is a hundred and twenty-seven pixels tall and permanent, which is fifteen per cent of a phone screen given up on every page they publish. Ours is allowed on a place page and on the itinerary and nowhere else, because a told place has nothing to pin and a hundred and ninety of our pages are told places.
