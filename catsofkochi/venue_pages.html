Aspinwall House

Fort Kochi · a 19th-century trading compound

Aspinwall was a coir, pepper and timber business, and the compound is the largest surviving piece of that trade in Fort Kochi. It is the main Biennale venue and the reason most people have heard of it.

⭐ The Guardian's nearest relative is a restaurant review — and it does the opposite of a facts strip

Measured on a current review (Lennox, Manchester — 787 words, zero subheads, one continuous piece). The practical facts are the last paragraph, in the body face at 17px, no panel, no labels, no date:

"Lennox Manchester, 60 Spring Gardens, Manchester M2 2BQ, 0161 832 1815; lennoxrestaurant.co.uk. Open Tues-Sun noon-midnight (1am Fri & Sat, 9pm Sun). About £70 a head a la carte for three courses (set menus from £35) plus drinks and service"

Guardian-faithful would mean no strip at all. I have kept it, and here are the two named reader differences — both on the licensed list:

  1. ⭐ Stranding facts. Their reader is choosing a restaurant at home, in advance, reading for pleasure. Ours is standing outside the building deciding whether to walk in now. Nobody has ever been stranded by a restaurant review.
  2. Their facts need no date because the review IS dated. It is published on a day and read as a snapshot of that night. A venue page is evergreen and gets read for years, so the same sentence would quietly become a lie.

⭐ One thing taken from them, and it is a real gap in my strip: the phone number. Their closing line carries one. Mine had hours, tickets and closed days and no way to ring ahead — which makes "call before you walk down" useless advice. Phone is now a strip row.

The two hard problems, designed for rather than papered over

1. The verdict may not exist. Seagull has one — Shemeer's own line, unedited, and it carries the whole page. Aspinwall has none, so it has no cat line at all. Not a filler one, not a generic one. The page simply starts with the writing. An empty cat line is the same defect as an empty facts row.

2. Unchecked is the launch state, not an edge case. AIK-79 is open, so most of these pages ship with more unknown than known. Both pages above show it: Seagull has one known fact and two honest gaps, and it still works, because "we have not checked the hours, call before you walk down" is more useful than a plausible guess.

Note the Seagull gap is written with the consequence attached — "it is not a short walk back". A refusal that ends with what to do is help; one that just admits ignorance is a shrug.

The three shapes, and what actually differs

  • A — Promote the existing article (4). ⛔ Prose untouched, URL untouched. Add the strip and, if a verdict exists, the opener. Nothing else. These are the site's best-ranking pages and the minimum change is the point.
  • B — Evergreen + dated season (10). As Aspinwall above. The building is the page; the Biennale is a block inside it that fills and empties. ⭐ Pepper House also carries a live what's on here list, because it runs year-round.
  • C — New short page (6). As Seagull. Verdict, a few sentences, strip, article list. ⛔ Do not invent an arc — a manufactured story is how 200-word stubs came back last time. Brevity is the honest state.

⭐ The layout is the same for all three. What differs is how much of it is filled in — which is the right kind of difference, because it means a thin page and a rich one are recognisably the same site rather than two designs.
