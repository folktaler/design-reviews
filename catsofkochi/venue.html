What we only know because we went

  • The holidays close it, and the listings do not say so

    Seven dates in 2026, on top of every Saturday. The community publishes them on its own site — and none of them reach the tourism listings, which is where almost everyone checks before walking over.

  • Friday is a short day, not a closed one

    It shuts at two on Fridays and stays open to six the rest of the week. Two of our own articles disagreed about this — one had it closed all day Friday — and the venue's own site settled it.

Why the building looks like that

Ginger here. Look down before you look up — the floor tiles came from China and no two repeat.

The synagogue sits at the closed end of a lane behind the Mattancherry Palace, and the first thing most people notice is that it is smaller than they expected. The clock tower is the tallest part of it and the tower is not the point. The point is the floor: several hundred hand-painted blue and white tiles, carried here from Canton in the middle of the eighteenth century, laid so that no two of them repeat. People walk in, look at the chandeliers, and walk out again without ever looking down.

It is worth arriving with time rather than with a plan. The room is small enough that ten minutes covers it and interesting enough that an hour does not, and the difference between those two visits is entirely a matter of whether anyone told you what to look at. The lane outside is a working street of spice godowns and antique dealers, and the walk in from the jetty is a large part of what you came for, even though nothing on the ticket says so.

Shemeer's verdict. Go in the morning, before the tour groups come through from the palace, and give it longer than it looks like it needs. If the door is shut when you get there, the walk back through the lane is not wasted time. — written by Shemeer, not drafted

Three decisions taken here, because the page is where they land

  • The content sheet does NOT rise over the photograph. The spacing tokens deferred this to "Phase 2's venue page and nothing else". Airbnb's sheet overlaps their hero by 23.5px with a 24px corner, and that one detail is most of why their page reads as an app. Refused, for two reasons: a guide is a document and not an app, and the overlap puts a text box on top of an image, where contrast cannot be computed from CSS colours at all — so the page could only ship by suppressing the check that would have caught it. A decoration that costs a gate is the decoration losing.
  • The hero is 16/9, not Airbnb's 1.05/1. Theirs is 44% of a phone screen before the reader learns anything, and their fold ends in a price. Ours has to end in an opening time. 16/9 is 211px on a 375px phone against 357px.
  • The cat line opens the writing, not the page. The locked page shape is H1 → cat → body → cat, and it was written for told pages, where the body is the point. On a venue page the reader came for an opening time, and twenty words of cat between the name and the hours costs them the answer. The closing cat line is dropped as well, because this section ends in the owner's verdict and a cat after it takes the last word away from the person whose judgement it is. ⚠️ Both need Shemeer's yes — they are the only two places this page departs from a locked document.

Two defects this page found in components that already shipped

  • The verification stamp was set as a caption. The theme tokens say amber marks the thing the reader came for, and that on a place page it is the verification stamp. FactsRow.astro rendered that stamp in secondary grey at 14px — the one element the rule names, set as the quietest thing in the row. Fixed in the component alongside this kit.
  • The trust-card number does not fit its cell. TrustCard.astro sets the value at 21px. Airbnb's measured equivalent is 18px. In a 342px card, three cells leave about 98px inside each one, and "3 Aug 2026" at 21px with tabular figures does not fit on one line. This kit sets 17px, the nearest step in our ladder to their measured value. The component needs the same change and has not had it.

What is deliberately not measured, and by what

  • The "not now" row's tap target. The checker's control selector reaches links in a nav, a header or a footer, and this row is in neither, so its 52px row overlay is unasserted. The failure it guards against is specific and invisible in a screenshot: two absolutely positioned overlays on one row, where the later one wins and a tap on the place opens the alternative instead. It needs an elementFromPoint probe at both ends of the row, shipped with the fault that breaks it.
  • The published bar height. The token above is the designed height and the script overwrites it with the measured one. If the script never runs, the layout is still right, which is exactly how a defence hides the thing it defends. Compare the custom property against the bar's own bounding box; they must agree.
