Why the building looks like that

Ginger here. Look down before you look up. Everybody comes for the ceiling and walks out over the floor.

The synagogue sits at the closed end of a lane behind the Mattancherry Palace, and the first thing most people notice is that it is smaller than they expected. The clock tower is the tallest part of it and the tower is not the point. By the community's own account the building went up in 1568, was destroyed by the Portuguese in 1662, and was rebuilt two years after that. People walk in, look at the chandeliers, and walk out again without ever looking down.

It is worth arriving with time rather than with a plan. The room is small enough that ten minutes covers it and interesting enough that an hour does not, and the difference between those two visits is entirely a matter of whether anyone told you what to look at. The lane outside is a working street of spice godowns and antique dealers, and the walk in from the jetty is a large part of what you came for, even though nothing on the ticket says so.

Shemeer's verdict. Go in the morning, before the tour groups come through from the palace, and give it longer than it looks like it needs. If the door is shut when you get there, the walk back through the lane is not wasted time. — written by Shemeer, not drafted

What this kit is, and what in it is not real

Everything a traveller would see is above this band. Everything below it is commentary about the mock and is excluded from every measurement, because measuring a designer's own notes as if they were the design is the proxy error this system exists to prevent.

The header — six elements, and three of them have a catch

Built 10 August 2026 to the six decisions the owner made one at a time. Wordmark only, with the space to its left held for the cropped cat mark. Three section tabs with the current one underlined. A search pill with two fields and a round button. Nothing in the right-hand text-link slot. A language globe. A menu at every width.

⚠️ What is inert

  • Both search fields and the search button do nothing. The front door has not been designed, and this page settles the control, not the panel behind it. They are ordinary buttons with no handler — not disabled ones, because a disabled control is greyed out and skipped by the keyboard, which is not what this will become. Every one carries data-inert="front-door-undecided".
  • Every link in the header points at #top. Places, Getting around, Writing, the wordmark, About, The monthly email and Contact are all stand-ins for pages that exist elsewhere or not yet.

⛔ The two dead ends, and what was done about each

  • A globe with one language behind it. The owner was told plainly that this reads as broken to whoever taps it and chose it anyway, so the work was to make the tap honest rather than to argue it again. The control is labelled English, so it states the truth before anybody touches it, and it opens on one sentence saying English is all there is. It makes no time claim — no "coming soon", no date — because languages ship over time by the owner's own ruling and any date here would be invented.
  • "How we check our facts" points at a page nobody has written. It is in the menu and it is not a link: a link to a 404 is worse than either alternative, and deleting the row would leave the menu quietly short of the one surface where this site is still allowed to explain why its hours disagree with a posted board. So the row says it is not written yet and carries the sentence that page would open with. It sits on the quiet surface so it cannot be mistaken for something tappable, and it is marked data-page-missing="how-we-check-our-facts" so the day the page lands it becomes an ordinary link.

⚠️ What it costs the phone, measured at 375 × 667

The header is 195px where the old one was 53px. The H1 moves from 288 to 430 and the first fact row moves from 424 to 566 — and the fixed action bar covers everything below 563, so on a 667px-tall phone the first opening-hours row is now behind the bar where before it was in plain sight. It is above the fold on a 390 × 844 phone. The header is deliberately not sticky, so this is a one-time scroll cost rather than a permanent one. ⭐ The single lever, quantified: the search pill row is 82px of that 142px increase and it is the part that does nothing yet. Held back until it works, the first fact row lands at 484 and stays visible on the small phone.

⛔ What on this page is real, and what is not

This list was inverted until 10 August 2026 and the inversion is worth recording. It declared the hours, the closing days, the entry price and the walk time "plausible and unverified" — and three of those four are real, sourced registry records. Meanwhile it said nothing at all about a trust card claiming we had visited, a section headed "what we only know because we went", and a paragraph of invented eighteenth-century provenance. A disclaimer that apologises for the true parts and stays silent on the invented ones is worse than no disclaimer, because a reviewer reads it and stops looking. All four inventions are now removed from the page; this is what is left.

Real, and sourced

  • The hours and the closing days are registry records. src/content/places/paradesi-synagogue.yaml — Sunday to Thursday 10:00–18:00, Friday 10:00–14:00, closed Saturday, plus thirteen dated 2026 holiday closures, all read off paradesisynagogue.com on 7 August 2026 and proved against tests/registry/paradesi-synagogue-hours.test.ts. The 7 August source line under each row is the real date.
  • The entry price really is unknown, and the refusal on the page is the record. The venue's own site says to ask staff; aggregators variously claim free, ₹5, ₹10 and ₹50; our own two articles say ₹10 and ₹100. entry_price is null and the row shows the missing state rather than picking one.
  • The walk time is routed, not walked. 4 minutes from Mattancherry Water Metro Station — Valhalla pedestrian costing over 0.31 km, computed 7 August 2026. It is a real measurement of a real distance and nobody has timed it with a phone in their hand. It was printed as "12 minutes from Mattancherry Jetty", which was neither.
  • The building's dates come from the venue's own site. 1568, destroyed 1662, rebuilt "two years later" — the site never names the rebuild year and neither does the page.
  • The step-free row is the honest one, and it is the one with no value in it. That is the grammar working.

Stand-ins — replace before this shape ships

  • The verdict. Shemeer's own words go here. The venue-verdict sheet has lines for David Hall, Pepper House, 111 Markaz, Bastion Bungalow and Aspinwall House, and none for this venue.
  • The hero photograph. The design system holds exactly one real photograph and it is of a water metro boat at the Fort Kochi jetty. The alt text is truthful about what is pictured. On the real page the hero is the synagogue.
  • The two description paragraphs are editorial placeholder in the right shape. story is null in the record on purpose — a sentence about what a place MEANS is not sourceable — so nothing here should be read as checked. ⚠️ Specifically: "ten minutes covers it and an hour does not" is a judgement, not a measured visit length. visit_minutes is NOT_FOUND.

Removed 10 August 2026, because it was invented

  • The trust card. Three cells: "3 Aug 2026 / last checked" (the record says 7 August, and the page said so two rows above), "We went / and looked ourselves" (nobody has), "1 of 5 / posted facts were wrong" (this site holds exactly one posted-versus-observed fact and it is Bastion Bungalow). The most persuasive component on the page was the least true one.
  • "What we only know because we went." Both remaining rows came off the venue's website at a desk, and both facts were already in the facts strip. One of them was also wrong: Kerala Tourism's listing does mention Jewish holidays — it omits the dates.
  • The Canton tiles. A count, an origin, a century and a pattern claim in one sentence, none of them sourced. The registry header names this sentence, in an earlier wording, as the invented copy that started this whole discipline.
  • Two "things to know" rows — "photographs not allowed inside" and a dress code. Probably true of a synagogue; not known to be true of this one.

Three decisions taken here, because the page is where they land

  • The content sheet does NOT rise over the photograph. The spacing tokens deferred this to "Phase 2's venue page and nothing else". Airbnb's sheet overlaps their hero by 23.5px with a 24px corner, and that one detail is most of why their page reads as an app. Refused, for two reasons: a guide is a document and not an app, and the overlap puts a text box on top of an image, where contrast cannot be computed from CSS colours at all — so the page could only ship by suppressing the check that would have caught it. A decoration that costs a gate is the decoration losing.
  • The hero is 16/9, not Airbnb's 1.05/1. Theirs is 44% of a phone screen before the reader learns anything, and their fold ends in a price. Ours has to end in an opening time. 16/9 is 211px on a 375px phone against 357px.
  • The cat line opens the writing, not the page. The locked page shape is H1 → cat → body → cat, and it was written for told pages, where the body is the point. On a venue page the reader came for an opening time, and twenty words of cat between the name and the hours costs them the answer. The closing cat line is dropped as well, because this section ends in the owner's verdict and a cat after it takes the last word away from the person whose judgement it is. ⚠️ Both need Shemeer's yes — they are the only two places this page departs from a locked document.

Two defects this page found in components that already shipped

  • The verification stamp was set as a caption. The theme tokens say amber marks the thing the reader came for, and that on a place page it is the verification stamp. FactsRow.astro rendered that stamp in secondary grey at 14px — the one element the rule names, set as the quietest thing in the row. Fixed in the component alongside this kit.
  • The trust-card number does not fit its cell — and the card is no longer on this page, so this is now a finding about the component alone, with no live example to point at. TrustCard.astro sets the value at 21px. Airbnb's measured equivalent is 18px. In a 342px card, three cells leave about 98px inside each one, and "3 Aug 2026" at 21px with tabular figures does not fit on one line. 17px is the nearest step in our ladder to their measured value. The component still needs that change and has not had it, and the only record in the registry that could honestly fill the card is Bastion Bungalow — so whoever draws it next should draw it there.

⚠️ A system finding this page surfaced and did not fix

The measure token and the measure ceiling disagree, and they always have. --measure: 32rem is 512px, and at this system's body size that renders 66 to 69 characters — against a stated ceiling of 65 in scripts/check-kits.mjs. Worse in a smaller cell: the things-to-know value was 16px in the same 512px box and reached 74, because a character count belongs to a type size, not to a pixel width. ⭐ Owner ruling, 10 Aug 2026: the ceiling becomes 70 — the 512px column is measured off Airbnb and deliberate, 66–69 characters sits inside the readable band, and nothing on any page moves. The 74-character cell was NOT absorbed by that: it is fixed on its own merits by raising it from 16px to 17px, where it measures 70 and matches the facts value beside it.

⛔ This is not caused by the responsive change and is not fixed by it. The prose column is 512px before and after — measured on the published page at 1440 before any of this landed. What the change did was make it visible, because a desktop width was never looked at before. It reads as clean today only because the checker gates the median line length, not the longest one.

Two honest options, and both are system-wide across nineteen kits rather than one page's call: narrow --measure until it delivers the 65 it promises, or raise the ceiling to the number the token actually produces and say so. ⭐ Capping individual components in ch was tried and rejected — it papers over a token that disagrees with its own rule, which is the failure this project already has a name for.

What is deliberately not measured, and by what

  • The "not now" row's tap target. The checker's control selector reaches links in a nav, a header or a footer, and this row is in neither, so its 52px row overlay is unasserted. The failure it guards against is specific and invisible in a screenshot: two absolutely positioned overlays on one row, where the later one wins and a tap on the place opens the alternative instead. It needs an elementFromPoint probe at both ends of the row, shipped with the fault that breaks it.
  • The published bar height. The token above is the designed height and the script overwrites it with the measured one. If the script never runs, the layout is still right, which is exactly how a defence hides the thing it defends. Compare the custom property against the bar's own bounding box; they must agree.
