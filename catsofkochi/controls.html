Both are right — they are different sites. The deciding argument is how many controls there are, not taste. The Guardian needs a shape system because their reader is constantly asking “can I press that?”. This site has about four controls in total.

Square is structure. Round is a person or a control.

Two shapes and a circle. That replaces the earlier “square everything except portraits” — the exception is now a reason, not a list.

Buttons

⭐ Their Sign-up button is an outline, not a solid block — transparent fill, a 1px ink border, 14px bold, radius 24px. Easy to miss, and it matters: on a page made of writing, a solid block shouts. An outlined pill reads as a control without raising its voice.

Ours — every variant and state

Outline is the default. The amber fill is the only solid button, and there should be about one per page.

The padding is lopsided on purpose — one extra pixel at the bottom, copied from theirs, because capital letters sit optically high inside a pill. It is the sort of detail that reads as made rather than generated.

Hover darkens the ground and never the label. Focus is a 3px ring — theirs is a blue halo at rgb(0,119,182); ours is the blue we already verified at 5.9:1.

It is not in a box. Theirs is transparent with no border and no padding — it sits in the page. A boxed signup on a page of writing reads as an advert, and an advert is the one thing every reader has trained themselves to skip. Ours gets a hairline above and below and nothing else.

⚠️ One thing only you can decide: how often. The cadence is required — the build refuses a signup without one.

Do not promise a frequency the site cannot keep. “Every week” that becomes every six weeks is a broken promise sitting in 1,408 inboxes. If the honest answer is “when something changes”, write that — it is a better promise because it can be kept.

The label above the field is visible, not a placeholder — a placeholder disappears the moment somebody types, which is exactly when a person using a magnifier needs it. And the privacy line is a promise, so it is quiet but not set in the grey of fine print.

Pull quote

A rule above and below, not a box and not a giant quotation mark. ⛔ Never a fare or a time — a fact pulled out of the body is a fact with no checked date on it. The build refuses those.

Inline links — the underline is quieter than the word

Measured in their body text: the link is coloured, has no underline, and instead carries a light grey rule underneath. A full-strength underline at body weight cuts through the descenders of every link in a 2,000-word memoir. This link uses their mechanism — the colour names it, the quiet rule confirms it, and hovering brings the rule up to full strength.

What we refuse

Their section colours. The Guardian tints headlines, links and pull quotes by section — measured purple rgb(101,0,84) on Travel, red rgb(199,0,0) on News. It is elegant, and it is already cut by your own direction document.

They are a newspaper with sections a reader moves between. You are one place, told by one voice. Answer pages and told pages are two rhythms of one publication — colouring them apart would make them two sites. One accent, and it means “we checked this”.

Also refused: the way they switch focus rings on and off with JavaScript. That solved a problem the browser now solves by itself.
