⭐ The confirmation page is the highest-risk copy on this site

Every commerce confirmation in the world says "Your order is confirmed." "Confirmed" is on the banned list (COMMERCE-TERMS.md §2.2) — alongside book, reserve, we'll arrange, your booking, guaranteed, on your behalf, we've held it for you. Those words create an agency relationship in a reader's mind and eventually in a complaint, and they would make this business a travel agent, dragging trip-level liability onto a fee-sized service.

The resolution: confirm the purchase, never the trip.

⛔ "Your booking is confirmed" · "We've arranged your days" · "Your reservation at…"
✅ "Paid. Your plan is being written." · "Nothing is held or arranged for you anywhere, so there is nothing to chase."
⚠️ A trap I walked into while writing this, worth passing on. My first version of that reassurance read "…arranged anywhere on your behalf" — and "on your behalf" is itself on the banned list. Used in the negative it is safe in meaning, but the build-time check in Unchecked.astro and NewsletterSignup.astro does substring matching and would have rejected the single most valuable sentence on the page.

⭐ The fix is the copy, not the rule. "arranged for you anywhere" says the same thing, passes any checker, and is plainer English. A rule that can be obeyed by writing plainly beats a rule with exceptions — and a checker that tried to parse negation would get it wrong eventually.

So: state the negative without borrowing the banned phrase. Whoever writes the real page copy will hit this, because the safest-sounding disclaimers are built out of exactly those words.

⭐ And that sentence does double duty. It is the legal boundary and the most reassuring thing on the page — a buyer who wondered whether they still have to organise something now knows. The safe wording turned out to be the more useful wording, which is the same thing that happened with the cat tip's rhythm, not schedule rule.

What the Guardian could teach, and what it could not

They do not sell itineraries, but they ask for money constantly and well. Measured on their contribute page — the mechanics worth taking:

  • Say who you are and what the money does before naming a price. Their opening line is an ownership claim, not a plea.
  • Spell out the "then what". They show the introductory price and what it becomes afterwards, in the same breath. Here the equivalent is stating how long the page stays live — which COMMERCE-TERMS.md §2.1 requires anyway, because without it the obligation never ends.
  • Currency is explicit. Theirs is switchable; ours says the card is charged in INR, which is the commonest dispute avoided.
  • One figure, plainly. A single total, not a build-up.
  • The pay button says what it does — "Pay ₹0,000", not "Continue". EU rules require the order button be unambiguous.

⛔ What does not transfer: the discount framing. Their ask is a recurring donation with an introductory rate. This is one payment for one trip — a struck-through price and a countdown would read as pressure on somebody spending real money on a holiday, and pressure is the opposite of the trust this product is sold on.

⭐ The page on the day it rains is the whole argument

FRESHNESS-SPEC.md §4: "A generated itinerary is frozen at the moment it was made. A living itinerary knows it rained." Someone buys in September for a November trip and a red alert lands the day before they arrive — a PDF is now a liability, confidently sending them to a boat that is not running.

So the third screen above is not a nice-to-have, it is the thing being sold, and the design says so by showing the struck-out plan next to its replacement. The reader can see the page changed its mind. A free tool structurally cannot do that — it does not know who you are or when you are travelling.

Note the checked-on stamp appears here too, dated to the day it was rewritten. It is the same component as everywhere else, and this is where it is worth the most.

⚠️ Four questions I could not answer, for Shemeer

  1. What does it cost, and is it per trip or per person? The page currently shows ₹0,000 and says "for the whole trip, however many of you" — that is the version I would defend, because per-person pricing invites haggling over a document.
  2. When the weather turns, does the page change itself, or does he change it? This decides whether the product is software or a service with his time in it — and therefore what he can promise about how fast.
  3. How long does the page stay live? I have written "until seven days after your last day". COMMERCE-TERMS.md §2.1 requires a stated end; the number is his.
  4. Does the buyer get anything to keep? A live page is the product, but somebody on a boat with no signal has nothing. If the answer is yes, it needs designing — and it must not be a PDF that outlives its own accuracy.

Refunds are not on this list — COMMERCE-TERMS.md §1.6 already has publishable text, and the page links to it.
