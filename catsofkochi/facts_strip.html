The checked-on stamp, three ways

Every fact on this site is going to carry the date somebody checked it, and whether we stood outside the door or only read the venue's own website. Three ways that could look on a phone, each shown twice — once where we know the posted board is wrong, once where we have only read a website.

Nothing here is approved. A, B and C are alternatives, not stages. The decision is one of them, or a change to one of them, or none of them.

Nothing here is invented. Both places are real records in src/content/places/. Bastion Bungalow is the one place on the site where we know the posted hours are wrong. The synagogue's hours came from its own website and nobody from here has stood there.

We have never gone to a place and found the board was right. That state is shown at the foot as wording only, labelled, rather than pinned on a venue that has not earned it.

What this page is, and what in it is not real

Everything above this band is what a traveller would see. Everything below it is commentary about the mock and is excluded from every measurement, because measuring a designer's own notes as if they were the design is the proxy error this system exists to prevent.

⛔ Nothing on this page is approved

A, B and C are three answers to the same question. AIK-90 is open and the choice has not been made. A reviewer's job here is to pick one, ask for a change to one, or reject all three — not to assume B is the plan because it is argued for below.

Where every fact on this page comes from

  • Bastion Bungalow — src/content/places/bastion-bungalow.yaml, hours_status: observed_contradicts_official. The Kerala Department of Archaeology's own page posts "Visiting Time: 9.00-1.00, 2.00-4.30"; the owner went in person and found it opens at 10:00. This is the site's only live instance of the moat, and the reason the whole component exists.
  • Paradesi Synagogue — src/content/places/paradesi-synagogue.yaml, hours_status: official_only, read from the venue's own site on 7 August 2026. ⛔ Its record says plainly that nobody has stood there, and this page says the same. A lower-tier source disagreeing is not an observation disagreeing.
  • The entry price is missing on purpose in both places. Four sources disagree about the synagogue's, two of them our own live pages. Printing a number here would put a figure nobody can source under a checked stamp, which is the exact failure this project has already had once.
  • The photographs are placeholders. No photograph of Bastion Bungalow exists in the repo. ⛔ Real images only, never AI-generated — an owner constraint, so the slot stays empty and says so.

⭐ The two verbs, and why only one of them is amber

The owner's own correction on 9 August 2026 — "provenance can be subtler, look how Airbnb does it" — is what sets the ranking here, and it is the same split venue.html already ships:

  • "We went and looked" is a stamp. Two or three words and a date, once per place, amber fill. It is the site's signature and the one thing amber is for.
  • "We read their own site" is a citation. A sentence naming a source, 14px, secondary grey, no fill. Reading is a weaker claim and it must look like one.

⛔ So the "we read" case has no stamp at all in variants B and C. Giving it an outlined box would have made the weaker claim wear the stronger one's clothes, which is what the owner had already rejected once.

What each shape costs, and one cost is measured

  • A, the shelf. The most scannable of the three. ⚠️ But three cells in a real 327px page column leave about 109px each — the number venue.html already records — and inside the specimen frame on this page they measure 97px. Either way "How we know" wraps to two lines and so does the date. Measured in Chrome at 375px, not estimated. Airbnb carry the same component because their labels are 10–12px; ours may not go below 14px, for a reader in Kerala sun. And the disagreement — the thing nobody else has — ends up as the last line in a box.
  • B, the correction. The disagreement is the headline. Costs more vertical space than A, and on a place with nothing surprising to report the stamped line is a lot of ceremony for "open ten to six".
  • C, the dateline. Easiest to read outdoors, sounds most like the site. ⚠️ But somebody skimming for an opening time scrolls straight past a sentence — there is nothing for the eye to catch.

The recommendation is B, on one ground: it is the only shape where the thing that makes this site different is the largest thing on the screen.

⛔ State 4 is the design problem, not an edge case

23 of 26 records have no hours. A strip that only looks right with hours in it would look broken on 88% of the site. So the refusal is set at body size in primary ink, the same weight as the facts around it — the moment it is set smaller or greyer, the site starts to look like it is hiding something. The refusal also ends with what to do next, because a refusal that just admits ignorance is a shrug.

What is deliberately absent

  • Stars, scores and review counts. Airbnb have a crowd; this site has one person who walks there. A number would be borrowed authority.
  • A green tick or a red cross. Colour cannot carry the difference between "we went" and "we read" for a reader who cannot see it, and red is the grammar of an error — a door being shut is not an error.
  • The words "verified" and "correct". The claim is about a process, not about the world. We can say what we did and when; we cannot promise nothing has changed since Tuesday.
  • A sticky action bar. Nothing on this page is a decision between two things, and a bar would cost 15% of a phone screen for nothing.

What has been measured, and what has not

  • Measured in real Chrome: standards mode (document.compatMode === "CSS1Compat"), no horizontal overflow at 375px, every tap target at least 44px on both axes, and the 108px shelf cell above.
  • Not measured: nobody has read this page on a real phone in real Kerala sun, which is the only test that finally matters and cannot be run from here.
