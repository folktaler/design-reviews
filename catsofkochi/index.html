Cats of Kochi — the Airbnb-faithful system

Labelled catsofkochi-airbnb-design-system-2026-08-09 — a different system from the earlier Guardian-era one (guardian-design-system-v1), stated here so nobody reviews the wrong one by name. 14 pages, one shared faces file per font family, no build step and almost no JavaScript. 13 of the 14 kits now show the current typeface, Figtree. One does not, deliberately — read the gap below before judging its type.

Known gaps — read this before reviewing

  • ⭐ facts_strip is now a decision, not a comparison. It used to show three competing proposals (A the shelf, B the correction, C the dateline). The owner resolved it on 9 August 2026: none of them — full hide. The page shows our hours plainly and says nothing about method, dates or the posted board. ⚠️ Anyone reviewing it against an older note about "A, B and C" is reading a superseded page. Its one deliberately fictional specimen ("we went and the board was right") is gone — under full hide it would have rendered identically to the real ones, so there was nothing left to invent.
  • ⚠️ Full hide creates a page that does not exist yet. Every venue page now carries one footer link, How we check our facts, and that is the only surface left where the site can explain why its hours differ from a posted board. Nothing is written behind it. Until it is, the link goes nowhere and the explanation lives nowhere.
  • ⭐ type_before_after still shows Noticia Text, and stays that way on purpose. The owner decided, 9 August 2026: Cats of Kochi is Figtree, all-sans — no second family (the other twelve kits carry that decision as of today; they were rebuilt from the Guardian-era serif this same day). This one kit is different in kind, not just behind: it is a dated 5 August essay arguing for Noticia Text — file-size measurements against eight fonts, a check for the ₹ glyph, a Guardian-line-spacing table — not a live specimen. Repointing its face without rewriting that whole argument would make the essay describe a font it no longer shows, which is worse than leaving it as history. Its listing below says so.
  • The Android fallback arm is unverified by rendering. Fallback metrics for Noto Serif and Roboto are derived from @capsizecss's published metrics, not measured on an Android device — nobody here has one to measure against. Measured on macOS 10 August 2026: the Helvetica Neue and Arial arms resolve; the Roboto arm reports error, which is the expected reading and not a defect — every fallback face is declared src: local(...), and macOS does not ship Roboto. Android is the platform where that arm is supposed to resolve, and it is the one neither reviewer can see from here. ⭐ If that face ever read loaded on a Mac, THAT would be the finding: it would mean local('Roboto') had matched something that is not Roboto.
  • This site renders in standards mode; the Claude Design preview does not. Every kit published there reports document.compatMode: "BackCompat" (quirks) because that host's serve wrapper injects markup ahead of the doctype — not our markup, not fixable there. Where the two differ, trust this one.

Changelog

  • ⭐ 10 Aug 2026 (latest) — three owner decisions, applied.
    • The cat tip block is rebuilt — answer_page, told_page, told_page_listing. The owner flagged it on all three pages and said only "not airbnb faithful", so the diagnosis was ours to make. Four faults, and the fourth is the one that mattered: the cat outranked the fact. "TIP FROM GINGER" was the header — a paw glyph, uppercase, letter spacing, a link — and the useful sentence sat underneath as body text. That is the narrator position, and the house rule is that cats are a byline, never the narrator. The other three: a paw glyph at all (banned outright, 5 Aug); a 13px uppercase label with 0.78px tracking (measured — Airbnb has none of this across 463 text elements, and the identical treatment was killed on the Checked chip on 9 Aug and never fixed here); and a 4px leading rule with a 20px inset, which set the tip's text at 488px against a 512px paragraph — 24px narrower than the column it sat in.
      Now: the fact leads at body size in medium weight, and the cat signs it underneath in ordinary grey. Two hairlines, top and bottom, which is Airbnb's one measured separator and the same shape as the pull quote. No paw, no tracking, no inset.

    • The clock format is the owner's: 10am – 1pm, 2 – 4.30pm. Lowercase, no space before the meridiem, no minutes on a round hour, a full stop rather than a colon, and the meridiem dropped from the first half of a range when both halves share it. Applied to every kit that prints an opening time, and to the live-clock formatter in today, which used to print "9.00 am".
      ⚠️ It costs the alignment argument, so it is written down rather than discovered later. "10am – 1pm" and "2 – 4.30pm" are not the same width, so a stack of opening hours down a listing page no longer forms the clean column a stack of "10:00–13:00" did. Tabular figures stay on — they still stop the numerals jittering, and the fares still need them — but they can no longer square an hours column. The specimen in system now says so.

  • ⭐ 9 Aug 2026 — a new page, reviews-workflow: visitor reviews, proposed. A feature that does not exist — no code, no ticket, nothing built. Three screens drawn against two owner decisions taken the same day: anyone with an account may review any place (no proof of visit, unlike Airbnb), and a person reads every submission before it appears.
    • The threshold is four reviews within two years, not Airbnb's three. Their three is bought — only a completed paid stay unlocks a review, so faking one costs a booking. A free account is cheaper, so the bar should be stricter, not looser. Four is also where one outlier stops deciding the number (a maximum swing of 1.00 stars against 1.33 at three), and five would mean the score never appears on a site with 26 places. Below the threshold the reviews print in full and the average is withheld, with the number that would make one appear named on the page — Airbnb's own answer to a low count, and the single most transferable thing in the reviews study.
    • No amber anywhere near a review. Amber means "we checked this" and a review is the one thing on the page nobody here checked. Exactly one amber underline exists on the whole page, in the moderation queue, pointing away from a review at the hours record it supports. Six Airbnb category ratings cut to two, the histogram cut, and the sort control cut with a warning attached: their default ordering is personalised to the reader, which is the one thing a facts registry could copy by accident.
    • ⚠️ Four defects were found only by rendering it — a stray hairline through every question on the form (a legend is laid out inside its fieldset's border), star rows indented 11px against the column, three queue tabs needing 298px inside a 285px card, and a breakdown bar chart whose 4px ink fill collided with --rule-leading and read as furniture. All four are invisible in the source.

  • ⭐ 9 Aug 2026 (later) — the colour-usage review, part two: links, the payment button, red, and the checked stamp. Five more findings from the same owner review, fixed across every kit, the shared components and tokens/theme.css, then reconciled onto the live site (src/styles/global.css):
    • Amber removed from every ordinary link — nine kits had a { color: var(--amber-ink) }, which painted every link amber and stopped the colour meaning anything.
