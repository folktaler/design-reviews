Cats of Kochi — the Airbnb-faithful system

Labelled catsofkochi-airbnb-design-system-2026-08-09 — a different system from the earlier Guardian-era one (guardian-design-system-v1), stated here so nobody reviews the wrong one by name. 14 pages, one shared faces file per font family, no build step and almost no JavaScript. 13 of the 14 kits now show the current typeface, Figtree. One does not, deliberately — read the gap below before judging its type.

Known gaps — read this before reviewing

  • ⭐ facts_strip is now a decision, not a comparison. It used to show three competing proposals (A the shelf, B the correction, C the dateline). The owner resolved it on 9 August 2026: none of them — full hide. The page shows our hours plainly and says nothing about method, dates or the posted board. ⚠️ Anyone reviewing it against an older note about "A, B and C" is reading a superseded page. Its one deliberately fictional specimen ("we went and the board was right") is gone — under full hide it would have rendered identically to the real ones, so there was nothing left to invent.
  • ⚠️ Full hide creates a page that does not exist yet. Every venue page now carries one footer link, How we check our facts, and that is the only surface left where the site can explain why its hours differ from a posted board. Nothing is written behind it. Until it is, the link goes nowhere and the explanation lives nowhere.
  • ⭐ type_before_after still shows Noticia Text, and stays that way on purpose. The owner decided, 9 August 2026: Cats of Kochi is Figtree, all-sans — no second family (the other twelve kits carry that decision as of today; they were rebuilt from the Guardian-era serif this same day). This one kit is different in kind, not just behind: it is a dated 5 August essay arguing for Noticia Text — file-size measurements against eight fonts, a check for the ₹ glyph, a Guardian-line-spacing table — not a live specimen. Repointing its face without rewriting that whole argument would make the essay describe a font it no longer shows, which is worse than leaving it as history. Its listing below says so.
  • The Android fallback arm is unverified by rendering. Fallback metrics for Noto Serif and Roboto are derived from @capsizecss's published metrics, not measured on an Android device — nobody here has one to measure against.
  • This site renders in standards mode; the Claude Design preview does not. Every kit published there reports document.compatMode: "BackCompat" (quirks) because that host's serve wrapper injects markup ahead of the doctype — not our markup, not fixable there. Where the two differ, trust this one.

Changelog

  • ⭐ 9 Aug 2026 (latest) — a new page, reviews-workflow: visitor reviews, proposed. A feature that does not exist — no code, no ticket, nothing built. Three screens drawn against two owner decisions taken the same day: anyone with an account may review any place (no proof of visit, unlike Airbnb), and a person reads every submission before it appears.
    • The threshold is four reviews within two years, not Airbnb's three. Their three is bought — only a completed paid stay unlocks a review, so faking one costs a booking. A free account is cheaper, so the bar should be stricter, not looser. Four is also where one outlier stops deciding the number (a maximum swing of 1.00 stars against 1.33 at three), and five would mean the score never appears on a site with 26 places. Below the threshold the reviews print in full and the average is withheld, with the number that would make one appear named on the page — Airbnb's own answer to a low count, and the single most transferable thing in the reviews study.
    • No amber anywhere near a review. Amber means "we checked this" and a review is the one thing on the page nobody here checked. Exactly one amber underline exists on the whole page, in the moderation queue, pointing away from a review at the hours record it supports. Six Airbnb category ratings cut to two, the histogram cut, and the sort control cut with a warning attached: their default ordering is personalised to the reader, which is the one thing a facts registry could copy by accident.
    • ⚠️ Four defects were found only by rendering it — a stray hairline through every question on the form (a legend is laid out inside its fieldset's border), star rows indented 11px against the column, three queue tabs needing 298px inside a 285px card, and a breakdown bar chart whose 4px ink fill collided with --rule-leading and read as furniture. All four are invisible in the source.
    Verified in real Chrome at 375px before publishing: document.compatMode === "CSS1Compat", zero horizontal overflow at page and container level, all 46 controls at least 44px on both axes, --tap and --measure resolving from their definitions rather than their fallbacks, line length median 42 and maximum 62 characters, and the type confirmed as real Figtree by bitmap comparison against both a metric-matched fallback and a family nothing declares. ⛔ Not verified: nobody has read it on a phone in Kerala sun, and no traveller has been asked whether they would review a place they did not pay to enter — which is the assumption the whole feature rests on. Pending owner reaction. Not approved, not built.
  • ⭐ 9 Aug 2026 — the checked-on stamp is settled: full hide. The owner's decision, and it removes rather than adds: "the end user need not see anything about how the facts are there. except maybe a small link on the general process we use. thats all. google maps does not do that. why should we?" facts_strip has been rebuilt as one finished design and no longer offers a choice.
    • What a traveller sees. The hours, at the largest size in the facts block, with the day range above them and the shut days below in ink. No stamp, no chip, no "checked on", no "the board says", no source line, no amber anywhere on the page. Where nobody knows the hours — 23 of the 26 records — the page says "We don't know when this opens" at body size in primary ink, then what to do instead.
    • One link, in the footer, once per page. How we check our facts — site-wide, not per venue and not per fact. It takes the ordinary link tier (ink, 1px grey underline), not the 2px amber decision tier: it advances no decision the reader came for, and amber on it would re-announce the provenance the page just stopped announcing.
    • ⚠️ The cost, stated plainly. A reader can no longer tell a time somebody stood outside and measured from a time read off a website — three registry states now render identically. The differentiator does not vanish, it moves: it is delivered as accuracy instead of announced as a badge. ⛔ This changes nothing in the registry. observed_contradicts_official is still immune from auto-correction — that is a data rule, and docs/FRESHNESS-SPEC.md and CLAUDE.md are untouched.
    • ⛔ What it leaves without a home: CheckedStamp and TrustCard on a venue page, and the data-fact-posted / data-fact-actual / data-checked-on / data-source-checked landmarks. data-fact="contradicts" and ="known" now render the same, so a scorer looking for a rendered contradiction will find none.

  • ⚰️ 9 Aug 2026 (superseded by the entry above) — facts_strip as three shapes awaiting a decision. Kept as history; the page no longer looks like this. The trust signal the site does not have yet: how a fact says who checked it, when, and whether the posted board disagrees. Three shapes, drawn on the two real records that carry hours — Bastion Bungalow (observed_contradicts_official, the site's only instance of the moat) and Paradesi Synagogue (official_only, read from the venue's own site). Built on the Airbnb trust-card grammar and explicitly not its machinery: no stars, no review counts, no "Guest favourite", because there is no crowd here to average.
    • The two verbs are ranked, and only one is amber. "We went and looked" is a stamp — two or three words, a date, an amber fill, once per place. "We read their own site" is a citation — 14px, secondary grey, no fill, the same .v-checked register venue.html already ships. This follows the owner's own 9 Aug correction that provenance was "too loud"; the weaker claim does not get the stronger one's clothes.
    • The refusal is designed for first, not last. 23 of the 26 place records have no hours, so the page shows all four states and sets the refusal at body size in primary ink — the same weight as the facts around it.
    • ⚠️ A measurement that counts against variant A: three trust-card cells leave about 109px each in a 327px column, so the cell labels and the date wrap to two lines. Airbnb carry the same component at 10–12px labels; this system's floor is 14px.

  • ⭐ 9 Aug 2026 (later) — the colour-usage review, part two: links, the payment button, red, and the checked stamp. Five more findings from the same owner review, fixed across every kit, the shared components and tokens/theme.css, then reconciled onto the live site (src/styles/global.css):
    • Amber removed from every ordinary link — nine kits had a { color: var(--amber-ink) }, which painted every link amber and stopped the colour meaning anything. Now a two-tier ladder ranked by underline weight, not hue: ordinary links get a 1px grey underline; the one link per screen that genuinely advances a decision gets a 2px amber one, capped at two. The live site's global.css had the same one-tier bug on its own a{} rule and now carries the identical contract, plus a .link--decision class for the amber tier (unused today — no correction/"report a change" link is built on the live site yet).
    • Pay ₹0,000 in the itinerary kit was amber-filled — the newsletter signup's colour, on the highest-stakes click on the site. Now ink, matching --action-primary-bg; rank stays legible without colour because both buttons are the same 52px pill height.
    • Red removed from non-error states, including an honest refusal — red is the grammar of an error, and "we don't know" or "shuts in 40 minutes" are not errors.
    • The "Checked" stamp and a provenance/source-citation line no longer share one amber fill — they were competing for the same signal. The citation now supports the stamp (ink text, underline) instead of duplicating it.
    • A landmark split: data-source-checked now marks a source-citation sentence separately from data-checked-on, which marks only a short, columnar date stamp — so an accessibility scorer checking tabular figures doesn't conflate "when we visited" with "when we read a citation."
    • Homepage's "Getting around" and "today" link-rows were showing a persistent underline under the link text stacked a few px above the row's own divider line — two grey lines read as clutter on every row. The divider already carries the separation; the underline now shows on hover only.
    Verified: npm run build and node scripts/check-kits.mjs — 177 passed, 0 failed, 31 documented skips.
  • ⭐ 9 Aug 2026 — a decorative amber wash, removed from every kit. The owner caught it live on this site: a facts block sitting in a solid amber-fill panel with a 4px amber border, next to a specimen arguing for Airbnb's actual style — near-white cards, thin grey hairlines, colour spent almost nowhere. It was real and repo-wide, not one kit's mistake: 12 instances across every page here, plus the live site's own .cat-line byline and call-to-action card, which carried the same fill (a milder, borderless form, but shipping). All now render background:var(--paper); border:1px solid var(--rule) — no wash, no accent border. The one deliberate amber fill that stays is the "Checked" stamp — a real verification signal, the one case theme.css's own rules call out as legitimate, and it reads clearly on the new near-white background where it nearly vanished on the old tinted one.
