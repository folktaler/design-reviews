⭐ Why the first control is not a search box

Airbnb's homepage opens with a search bar because their reader already holds the three inputs it wants — a city, some dates, a number of people — and the bar is a filter over a structured database, not a lookup. A traveller planning Fort Kochi holds one of those inputs and none of the vocabulary: they do not know the words "Pepper House", "Muziris Contemporary" or "Kayees" until after they arrive. A search box serves the reader who can already name the thing — and that reader arrives from Google on the page itself and never sees this one.

⭐ So the slot keeps Airbnb's job and changes its shape. The one input every visitor has is how long they are here, which is the top-ranked question in the demand study by a distance and is also the only real input the paid itinerary takes.

⛔ Unbuilt. "One day" / "Two days" / "Three or four" are three pages that do not exist. The repository holds one itinerary guide, not three. This control is a proposal, not a link.

⛔ What is still fake on this page

  • Every href is #top. Nothing navigates.
  • The today block's three places, hours and weather line are the same demonstration data as the previous homepage kit. The synagogue's Friday close at 2pm is real and sourced; the rain line is not.
  • No counts are printed anywhere. The "everything we keep current" link carries data-count-slot="answer_pages" instead of a number, because nobody has counted those pages and a number typed into a mockup becomes the number everybody quotes.
  • The three "first time" cards are a stance written here, not one the owner has approved.

⚠️ The photographs, and what is not known about them

All five are real photographs from the site's own Ghost library, opened and looked at, not selected from filenames. There is no provenance or licence record for any image in that library — the cache manifest is a 47-entry de-duplication map and records no photographer, no rights and no consent. Two specific risks the owner should know about:

  • The Pepper House photograph shows a Biennale installation that has since come down. It is honest about what the room looked like and dishonest about what you will find there today.
  • The Muziris Contemporary photograph is a photograph of another artist's work, and the snack cart photograph shows four identifiable visitors at close range.

⛔ The page weighs 1.3 MB, and 1.19 MB of that is five photographs

Measured over the wire, 10 Aug 2026: HTML 56 KB, the Figtree faces 55 KB, and then 411 + 290 + 236 + 167 + 86 KB of JPEG. These are the untouched Ghost originals — 2000px wide files being painted into a 327px column. The reader this site exists for is on mobile data in Kerala.

⭐ Nothing about the design causes this and nothing in the design fixes it: it needs the responsive image component DESIGN-DIRECTION.md already requires — correct sizes per device, modern formats, explicit width and height. At 375 CSS px on a 2× screen the largest file any of these needs to be is about 750px wide, which lands each of them near 40–70 KB. That is roughly 1.19 MB down to 0.25 MB, for no visible change. Until that exists, this page is a design proof and not a shippable homepage.

⭐ Six defects that only rendering found — the record

All six were invisible in the source, and several of them passed every check that was run before somebody looked at the page. They are listed so the next person to touch this kit does not reintroduce them, and so the cost of reviewing a design by reading its CSS is on the record.

  1. The lead headline rendered in browser-default #0000EE blue. The kit had no base a rule, and the lead card's <a> wraps the headline. It scored 9.16:1 — so a contrast audit passed it. Passing contrast and being the wrong colour are different questions. Fixed with one base rule; the venue kit learned the same thing on its "instead" link.
  2. A 4/3 lead photograph cut a third off both panoramas. 4/3 is Airbnb's measured card ratio, so it looked like the faithful choice. Both lead photographs in the library are 2.05:1 (2000×974 and 2000×980): a centre crop to 4/3 removed the left-hand fish seller from the mural and the two outer frames from the salon wall. ⭐ A grid needs a constant ratio; a single card does not. The lead now keeps the photograph's own shape.
  3. Four "other ways in" links were 19px tall. Inline links in a flowing sentence — the smallest targets on the page, in the band whose entire job is sending people somewhere. Padding cannot fix an inline <a> without its hit box overlapping the lines above and below. They became chips, the same component as the chooser.
  4. The "Instead:" link was 19px tall, and the first fix was worse than the defect. Giving the link inline-flex and a 52px minimum passed the tap check and opened a 30px hole through the middle of a two-line sentence. Rendering showed that; the measurement did not. The overlay moved out to the whole "Instead:" line, layered above the row's own overlay and probed with elementFromPoint at nine points.
  5. Leading drift at a single type size. 14px was rendering at 16.8, 19.25 and 21px and 16px at 22 and 24px, because some elements set a line-height and others silently inherited the body's 1.5. Nothing looks broken; the page just reads as unedited.
  6. A 96px square thumbnail was a stain, not a photograph. The Pepper House image is a dark gallery interior (mean luma 47). Cropped to 96×96 it was a brown-black rectangle, and the ceiling its own card line tells you to come for was invisible. Every measurement passed. The cards became Airbnb's full-width 4/3 card — measured at 245px for the photograph and ~360px for a whole card, so three of them are 1.3 screens, not the three screens the original objection assumed.

⚠️ And one trap in the checking, not the page. Four of the five photographs sit below the fold with loading="lazy", so on a first measurement they report naturalWidth: 0 — never requested, which is indistinguishable from a 404. The fail-loud guard below cannot fire on them either, because it hooks onerror and no request was made. Force every image eager before measuring, and read the canvas pixel range rather than trusting naturalWidth or a screenshot — two screenshots showed blank grey for images whose bitmaps were verifiably present.
