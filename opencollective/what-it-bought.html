What this page is for

It is the six-star moment in the shaping document, and the whole argument for eight weeks rather than three. Priya taps a link in an email, lands here already signed in, and meets one thing: what her ₹500 became, with the shop's paper above the gifts that paid for it.

The decisions on this page

  • The outcome comes before the money. A headline, a photograph and one sentence before a single number is broken down. She came for the drums, not for a statement.
  • The bill is a document, not a row in a table. Shop name, bill number, line items, the photograph of the paper, and who typed it in. A number without its paper is a claim; with the paper it is evidence.
  • The gifts did not cover the bill, and the page says so. ₹23,900 of ₹38,200. Hiding the ₹14,300 Aikyam put in would have been easy and would have been the first lie.
  • Amounts are shown, names never are — not even between people who gave to the same thing. Nobody decided that before now; it is decided here.
  • "Not spent yet" is a status she can read. Not pending, not processing. A donor who funds something slow is told nothing has happened, which is the truth.
  • PAN is offered as a certificate she can have, not a form she must fill. The sentence about 31 May is the plain warning the shaping document asked for.

Why there is no share button here

  • This page cannot be forwarded and should not pretend it can. It sits behind Priya's sign-in, and the organisation behind it is private, so a link she pastes into WhatsApp is a sign-in wall to everybody else.
  • The email is the only thing in this product that survives a forward, because it carries its own copy of the story. So the forwarding job moved there entirely — email-the-drums-arrived.html — and this page got simpler for it.
  • This page holds everything personal; the email holds none of it. Her amount, her three organisations, her receipts and her PAN live here, behind sign-in. That split is the rule, not a preference: see the email's own notes.

Where this leaves Open Collective's look, and why

  • Body type is 17px, not 14px. Open Collective's 14px is right for an admin scanning a dashboard all day and wrong for one person reading one thing once, on a phone.
  • Buttons a donor taps are 52px tall, not 44px. 44 is the floor, not a target, and this is a phone page.
  • Colour is spent on exactly one thing: her line, her amount, her share of the bar. Everything else is grey. Open Collective's blue is used as the accent it already is.
  • Every colour, radius, badge and button style otherwise comes from tokens.css unchanged. Nothing new was invented.

Known gaps

  • The photograph and the bill thumbnail are placeholders drawn in code. No real image is embedded.
  • No state for "she has given to one thing only" — the sections that compare across organisations would need rewriting for a first-time donor.
  • No state for "Aikyam has nothing to raise for right now", which makes the share block homeless. It should fall back to Aikyam's standing page; that page is not drawn.
  • The 80G certificate itself, the receipt PDF, and the PAN form are not drawn.
  • No email is drawn — and the email is half of this moment.
  • There is no JavaScript on this page at all, and nothing on it needs any. There was, briefly: a copy-link script left behind when the share block was cut, throwing on every load. It was found by opening the page rather than by reading it.
  • Line length in characters was not counted. Measured at 375px in a browser, nothing overflows sideways (0px) — but that is geometry, not typography.
