Not an email we send any more — it is what the update says

Open Collective sends the update email itself, to everyone who contributed. So this stops being an artefact we build and becomes the content and the tone for theirs: the words, the order, the bill, the call to action, and the rule at the bottom of this page. That rule is the most valuable thing here and it survives intact.

What is no longer ours: the wrapper. The from-address, the footer, the unsubscribe line and the "read in a browser" link belong to the platform. Design owns the body and the subject line, and nothing else.

One thing got easier and one got riskier. Easier: this page's open question — a permanent public web copy of each update — is answered by the public collective, so a forwarded reader lands on a real page rather than a locked door. Riskier: the personal-data rule below is now enforced by their email template, not ours. ⛔ Somebody must open one real update notification from the fork and check it carries no name, no amount and no sign-in token. If it does, the forwardable email is broken by the platform and nothing we write fixes it. That has not been checked.

Also stale: every raseed.in URL below. The address is doable.aikyam.fund, and the naming question is open — see naming.html. Nothing inside the email itself has been edited; it is kept as drawn.

What is ours now, and what is theirs

  • Ours: the body. Headline, story, bill, who paid, what is needed next. Written by a person at the organisation, in the organisation's voice, every time. An update that sounds like a platform does not get forwarded.
  • Ours: the subject line, and it is still one guess rather than a tested set. Three worth trying against each other: the one here, "Fourteen girls, six drums, one bill", and "What ₹23,900 bought in Fort Kochi".
  • Theirs: everything around it. From-address, footer, unsubscribe, the browser link, the plain-text version. Do not design these; check them.
  • Theirs, and it needs checking: the personal-data rule. The rule below used to be ours to keep. It is now a property of somebody else's template. ⛔ Open one real update notification from the fork and confirm it carries no name, no amount and no sign-in token. Unchecked.

The decision this page now forces

An Open Collective update can go to contributors only or to everyone. That single switch decides whether this email is forwardable at all — a contributors-only update is a locked door to whoever Priya sends it to, which is the exact failure the public collective just removed.

  • Recommendation: the update carrying a bill is public. The collective's page, its balance, its expenses and the bill are already public, so restricting the update gates nothing that is not one tap away — while costing the forward, which is the only growth this product has.
  • Contributors-only is for the intimate material: photographs of children, video, names, the voice note. The money is public; the people are not.
  • This is decision 1 in the shaping document and it is the Builder's, not mine to close.

What changed inside the email

  • The call to action got better. "Give to this" pointed at a page of ours that had to explain itself from nothing. It now points at the collective's own public page, which carries the ledger, the past bills and the story. A forwarded stranger arrives somewhere that proves itself.
  • "Gave to this already? Your own page" is redundant — the platform's own email already links her account. The principle it protected is not redundant: no sign-in token in anything designed to travel.
  • Every raseed.in URL is stale. The address is doable.aikyam.fund, and whether anything of ours is named in the footer at all is an open question.
  • The invented-people line in the footer stays while these pages are published, and so does the one on the collective's public page. Public means public.

The original notes, kept as they were

Written before the reversal and left unedited. The rule below is the durable part of this whole page — it was true when we were sending the email and it is true now that we are only writing it.

The rule this email exists to protect

An email designed to be forwarded contains nothing personal. Ever. No amount given, no name, no receipt, no PAN, no yearly total, and no sign-in link that carries a token. The moment Priya forwards it she is sending whatever is in it to people she has not thought about, and she will not know she did. Everything personal lives behind her sign-in, on her own page.

The tempting line is "your ₹500 helped buy these". It is warmer, it is the sentence the product is built on, and it must not be in this email — it broadcasts what she gave to a WhatsApp group of forty. That sentence belongs on her own page, where it is written in full and nobody else can read it.

Why this is not a thank-you

  • Nobody forwards a thank-you. A thank-you is about the person who got it, so passing it on is boasting. This email is about fourteen girls and a shop in Peruvanam, so passing it on is telling somebody something.
  • It assumes no prior knowledge and no relationship. No "thanks for your support", no "as you know", no reference to an earlier email. It explains who Aikyam is, what a chenda is, and why any of it matters, in the first two paragraphs.
  • The receipt email is the opposite and is a different email — sent minutes after she gives, all about her, carrying her amount and her sign-in link, and never designed to travel. It is not drawn here.

Who the call to action is written for

  • The stranger, because they are the only reader with anything left to do. Priya has already given. "Give to this" is aimed past her, at whoever she sends it to.
  • You cannot tell which one opened it, so the email does not try. Both lines are on the page, and both are true whoever reads them. The line for people who already gave sits below the button, quiet, and asks a question rather than assuming an answer.
  • The link is just the link. The giving page is public to anyone who has the URL, so a forwarded reader taps once and is in the flow. No account, no request for access, nothing to explain.
  • "Your own page" carries no sign-in token. It goes to a page that asks for an email address and sends a fresh link. A magic link in a forwardable email is somebody else's account arriving in a stranger's inbox — the one genuinely dangerous thing this email could do.

Built for a hostile medium

  • Tables, inline styles, no stylesheet, no JavaScript, no web font. It links neither tokens.css nor faces-inter.css; the tokens are a reference here, not a stylesheet.
  • Colours are the sRGB hex of the same colours the tokens name. The token file writes them in oklch, which no mail client understands, and annotates each one with its Tailwind name — so --primary is blue-600 #2563eb, the brand navy is #0c2764, and so on. Nothing was picked by eye.
  • The photograph is drawn as its images-off state, because a large share of readers see exactly that. The sentence inside the grey block is the alt text, and it is written to be worth reading on its own.
  • Order survives stripping. Read this email with every style removed and it still runs headline, story, bill, who paid, what is needed next, link. Nothing important is carried by a background colour.
  • Body text is 17px. Mail clients on phones shrink aggressively and this is the one thing that cannot be fixed after sending.
  • The bill's amounts are table cells, not floated spans. The first draft floated them right; measured at 375px the description wrapped and the unit price ended up sitting on the same line as the total, two rupee figures side by side with no way to tell which was which. Floats are unreliable in Outlook as well, so this was wrong twice.
  • The outer table is width:100%; max-width:600px, not width:600px. Measured at 375px the fixed version pushed 281px of the page off the right edge.

Known gaps

  • Not tested in any mail client. Not Gmail, not Outlook, not Apple Mail. The rounded corners and the padded link button are the two things most likely to render badly in Outlook, which needs a VML fallback this file does not have.
  • No real photograph is embedded, and no image hosting is decided. A hosted JPEG needs a public URL that will outlive the email.
  • The subject line is one guess, not a tested set. Three worth trying against each other: the one here, "Fourteen girls, six drums, one bill", and "What ₹23,900 bought in Fort Kochi".
  • No dark-mode treatment. Several clients invert colours themselves and will do something unplanned with the pale blue block.
  • The "read this in a browser" link implies a permanent public web copy of this email. That is a small widening of what is public and it needs a yes — see the giving page's notes.
  • The receipt email, the "we could not take your money" email, and the 80G-certificate-ready email are all unwritten.
