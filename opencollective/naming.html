What this is called

A recommendation, not a decision already taken. Vetoable in one line — but design owns naming here, so this is the answer rather than a shortlist.

raseed

Said "ra-SEED". Lower case, always — the way "open collective" is.

Give to an Indian organisation through raseed, and weeks later you are shown the shop's receipt for what your money actually bought.

Why this word

Raseed is the everyday Indian word for a receipt — Persian by origin, Hindi and Urdu by use, and borrowed into Malayalam, Bengali, Marathi and Telugu long ago. Priya knows it. Devi knows it. Nusrat knows it. Nobody has to be taught what it means, and that matters more than distinctiveness for a product that lives or dies on one stranger trusting it with ₹500 at 9pm.

It also names the thing this product actually does, which nothing else on the market does: it gives you somebody else's receipt. Every platform gives you your own — that is a solved problem and a commodity. This one shows you the shop's bill for the thing your money went into. The name and the differentiator are the same word, which is rare and worth taking.

And it survives the two sentences that have to be said out loud most often: "I gave through raseed", and "it's on raseed". Both are sayable by somebody who has never seen it written.

How to describe it, in order of length

Four words, for a WhatsApp forward or a button:

See what it bought.

One sentence, for a stranger:

Give to an Indian organisation through raseed, and weeks later you are shown the shop's receipt for what your money actually bought.

Two sentences, for Nusrat, deciding whether to bring her organisation:

Your money goes straight into your own bank account through your own payment account — raseed never touches it, and nothing about your organisation is public. The people who gave to you get a permanent page showing what their money bought, and nobody else can see a thing.

The words this product does not use

Never say

"Donate to us" / "your donation to raseed" — money never comes to us. It is always to Aikyam, into Aikyam's own bank account. Getting this wrong is not a tone problem, it is a claim about who holds the money.

"We issue your 80G certificate" — the organisation issues it, under its own registration. We keep it where she can find it. Say "Aikyam issues", every time.

"Tax-free" / "50% back" — a rate we have not verified for her circumstances. Say "can be claimed against Indian income tax" and stop there.

"Sign up", "register", "create an account" — she is given a page, not asked to join something. The word "account" appears nowhere on the giving page.

"Thank you for your support" — in the update email, ever. It makes the email about the reader, and an email about the reader is one nobody forwards.

"Transparent" / "accountability" / "impact" — sector words that mean nothing to a person on a bus. Show the bill instead.

Say instead

"Aikyam's own bank account." · "Aikyam issues the certificate." · "You are shown the shop's bill." · "No password, ever." · "Nothing is public — only the people who gave can look."

Two voices, and they are different on purpose

  • The update email is the organisation's voice. It is from Aikyam, about fourteen girls and a shop in Peruvanam. raseed appears once, in the footer, in a sentence explaining what it is. An email that sounds like a platform does not get forwarded.
  • Her own page is raseed's voice. Plain, exact, slightly administrative in the good sense — this is where amounts, statuses, receipts and PAN live, and where being precise matters more than being warm.
  • The giving page is Aikyam's voice with raseed standing quietly behind it, appearing only where a stranger needs to know who is handling the payment and why she is about to be given a page.

Where these pages leave Open Collective's look

Every colour, radius, badge, input and button comes from tokens.css unchanged, and the type is the Inter the platform self-hosts. Three deliberate departures, all for the same reason — these are consumer pages on a phone, and Open Collective's system is a dashboard system for people who use it all day:

  • Body type 17px, not 14px.
  • Tap targets 52px, not 44px.
  • One accent colour per page, spent on the money and the action, with everything else grey. Open Collective spreads its blue across navigation, links and chrome; on a page that has one job that dilutes the one thing worth looking at.

The email uses no tokens at all — an inbox cannot read oklch() or a custom property. It uses the sRGB hex of the same colours the token file names in its own comments.

What could not be checked

The name has not been cleared. No trademark search, no company-name check, no check of whether raseed.in, raseed.org or raseed.co.in is free. The mock pages use raseed.in as a placeholder URL and that is an assumption, not a finding — I have no way to run those checks from here.

The word itself I am confident about — it is a common loanword across Indian languages including Malayalam. Worth saying out loud to three people in Kochi before it goes on anything printed, because a name is only right if it is right in the room.

Geometry was measured at 375px; typography was not. All four pages were opened in a browser at phone width: none overflows sideways, and every tap target clears 52px. Line length in characters was never counted, so the column widths remain a judgement from the type scale rather than a measurement.
