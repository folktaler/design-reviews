## ⛔ CORRECTIONS — three fixes that were not fixes, found in the last hour

**1. `--af-measure` — settled at 552px, and the panic that produced it was based on a misreading.**
A designer reported 628 and 592 as failing on "worst line 68 vs a gate of 65", and three designers
independently converged on ~576px being unsafe. ⛔ **Read the source: `check-kits.mjs:959` is**
```js
const over = wide.filter((p) => p.median > MEASURE_MAX);
```
**It gates the MEDIAN line length per prose block, not the maximum line.** A single 68-character line
inside a block whose median is 60 does not fail. So the *worst-line* alarm was false.

⛔ **But my own correction was then corrected, and this is the part that matters.** Re-measured per
block, in real Noticia at 1024px, **against institutional copy**:

| column / body | institutional copy | gate |
|---|---|---|
| 552 / 19px · 552 / 20px | 55–62 | PASS |
| **592 / 20px** | 61–**66** | ⛔ **FAIL** |

**592px genuinely fails** — one paragraph on `impact.html` medians 66. So "nothing was failing" was
wrong too. It passes on the reading corpus and fails on the institutional one.

⭐⭐ **The real finding: a shared token validated against ONE corpus is not validated at all.** The
densest paragraph anywhere on the site sets the ceiling. Two designers each measured honestly, on
their own pages, and reached incompatible values.

⭐ And gating the median rather than the maximum is *correct*, not sloppy: the longest line grows with
how much text there is, so a max gate would measure **how long an article is** rather than how wide
the column is. A 1,900-word profile would fail a column that a 200-word page passes.

**SETTLED: 592px — the system's original value.** It moved twice and both moves were wrong.
⛔ **The "592 fails" case was built on a 157-character paragraph the suite never samples.** Lines
132–133: `PROSE_MIN_CHARS = 250`, `PROSE_MIN_LINES = 4`. Re-measured against the union of all three
corpora, per qualifying block, at 20px: highest median **62** against a gate of 65, nothing near the
sub-55 advisory. Two designers reached 62 independently on different copy with different harnesses.

⛔ **`--af-measure` and the body clamp are COUPLED and must be copied together.** At a hardcoded 19px
a 592px column medians 66 and fails. That state is unreachable by construction — a 592px column only
exists above a 640px viewport, where the clamp is already at 20px — but **a kit that copies the
measure and not the clamp breaks the coupling, and the failure is invisible in the source.**

⚠️ **When the suite runs, a green `measure` row will NOT be eleven confirmations.** Four of the five
institutional pages (`home`, `impact`, `contact`, `partners`) have no qualifying prose at all — every
paragraph is under 250 characters — so they report **`skip`**: *"this kit is a list or a specimen
sheet, not continuous text."* Only `about.html` and whichever reading pages carry real prose are
actually measured. ⛔ A row of skips must not be read as a row of passes — same shape as the
`134 passed, 0 failed` that once sat beside exit code 1.
⚠️ Consequence to expect: the body-size clamp was derived from a 592px column and reaches 20px at a
640px viewport, while the column now caps at 600px — a 40px band where type grows and the column does
not. Harmless, but visible, and it is the trace of this disagreement.
⭐⭐ **Two durable lessons from one argument.**
**(a) Read what the gate actually tests BEFORE measuring anything.** The rule was inferred from the
constant's *name* — `MEASURE_MAX` reads as "the longest line" and means "the maximum permitted
value". Same shape as counting `inst-slot` and catching the CSS rule that defines it: **twice in one
session, a name mistaken for a use.**
**(b) Careful measurement of the wrong quantity is still the wrong answer** — and so is a correct
measurement on an unrepresentative sample. Both happened here, in that order.

**2. Five reading pages were published with NO real typeface.** The EA checked for `base64` and
`@font-face` as two separate greps; both matched the *fallback* declaration, so five pages reported
as fine and were published rendering in a stand-in. Fixed — all eleven now verified with a single
pattern (`@font-face` naming Noticia Text *with* a base64 src).
⭐ **Georgia and Noticia are 4.9% apart on a 30-character string and 0.6% apart on a short one.**
That closeness is why a missing face survives review. **Any face check must measure a LONG string
against a BOGUS family name** — comparing against Georgia proves almost nothing, and
`document.fonts.check()` returns true for absent fonts.

**3. `--af-accent` is `#24164F` (deep indigo), not the placeholder green.** ⭐ **Ghost injects
`--ghost-accent-color` at RUNTIME** — genuinely absent from the stylesheet source, present in the
computed style. Searching the source and concluding "there is no accent" was reasonable and wrong.
**Read the computed value, never the source.** It also changed a rule, not just a hex: accent against
ink is **1.09:1**, so accent-as-text-colour was doing no work while looking like it did. Topic labels
are now the quiet grey, told apart by case and weight.

**4. The kits were light-only; the live site ships DARK by `prefers-color-scheme` with no toggle.**
They would have been white rectangles in a dark site. Both themes now rendered and looked at; lowest
contrast pair in either is 6.75:1. ⚠️ Six dark-mode values still need the foundation's sign-off.

**5. A count that measured a name, not a use.** "12 slot markers" came from grepping `inst-slot`,
which also matches the CSS rule that *defines* the component and its dark-mode override. Real count
via `class="inst-slot"` is **8**, and `about.html` has **zero** — an earlier version of this document
sent readers hunting for a marker that does not exist. ⚠️ **Any count produced by grepping a class or
token name is inflated by the rule that defines it.**

⭐ **The habit that caught most of this, worth adopting:** *"Adopting the foundation's 24px gutter
silently undid my nav fix — caught only because I now re-measure after EVERY token change, not just
the relevant-looking one."*

Also fixed: three dead served-path `@font-face` rules were still firing **3 × HTTP 404 per page load
before text paints**. Removed from all five institutional pages. Verified: zero dead faces across all
eleven.

## ⛔ Before anyone runs `check-kits.mjs` — three blockers

1. **It cannot reach this directory.** `KITS_DIR` is hard-coded to `<catsofkochi>/design-system/ui_kits`
   and it refuses any path not under the repo — a symlink 404s too. Each kit must be copied in as its
   own directory (URLs are `/kits/<name>/…`).
2. **`FAMILY_DEFAULT` is still `'Figtree'`** from the Airbnb era, so every Noticia kit is judged against
   the wrong family and `font-declared` reports a false red. ⚠️ The `KIT_FAMILY` override map is
   documented as migration **debt** — adding these five files a correct system to a debt list, and the
   next person will "pay it down" by deleting them. The honest fix is a per-project family.
3. ⭐ **Splice the faces BEFORE running it, not after** — otherwise the harness measures a different
   artefact than the one published. The `font-404` fault was already extended to rewrite base64 as well
   as filenames, so base64 in a kit is safe. ⛔ What is still dangerous is a `<link>` to a shared faces
   file: the fault rewrites the kit's HTML and never touches a linked stylesheet.

## ⛔⛔ Four defects, found by loading it into a browser

`system/verify.mjs` loads every stylesheet in the documented order into Chromium and reads
`getComputedStyle`. `node system/verify.mjs` (needs a global playwright). **All four were shipping.**

1. ⛔ **`--af-brand` was used 13 times and defined nowhere.** An undefined `var()` is invalid at
   computed-value time, which discards **the declaration, not the rule**. In `::selection` and
   `[data-topic]:hover` the `background` half was thrown away and `color: paper` survived.
   **Paper-coloured text on paper — selecting any text on any page made the words vanish.** It needed
   a mouse drag to appear, which is why a day of review never saw it.
   ⭐ Fixed by adding the semantic layer, which is where `--af-brand` should always have resolved.
2. ⛔ **`.prose a { min-height: 0 }` was eating the tap floor of standalone controls.** Specificity
   (0,1,1) beats `[data-topic]` at (0,1,0), so a topic tag or button inside `.prose` rendered
   **24.1px tall** against a 44px gate. Now `:where(.prose) a` — zero specificity, same reach.
   ⚠️ **Do not "tidy" the `:where()` away.** It is arithmetic, not decoration.
3. ⛔ **The masthead nav fix worked at 375px and failed at 320px** — two rows and a 191px masthead on
   a 568px screen, 34% of the display before a word of content. It had only ever been measured at the
   reference phone. 4px padding gets one row at both widths with 7px spare.
   ⭐ This is exactly the check the new `breakpoints.css` says to run: **overflow checks start at the
   floor, not at 375px.** The 7 Aug measurement below ("375px: 0 overflow…") was true and was not
   the test.
4. ⛔ **Footer links were 29.8px wide** with a correct 52px height — the width axis, missed for the
   third time across two projects.

**Verified after the fixes, Chromium at deviceScaleFactor 2:** 77/77 custom properties resolve · 219
rules parsed, no swallowed comment · `Noticia Text` actually painted · body exactly 20px at a 640px
viewport (the documented measure/type coupling holds) · **no tap target under 44px on either axis** ·
one nav row and zero horizontal overflow at **both 320px and 375px** · masthead 137px.

⚠️ **This proves the CSS computes. It does not prove a page looks right.** The fixture is a component
sheet, not a designed page. A tester verdict is still owed.

⭐ **Defects 2 and 4 are the fifth and sixth of the family listed under "found by looking".** Six in
two days, every one invisible in a source review. The pattern is not *read more carefully* — it is
**load it and measure it.**

## ⛔ The fork that is now named

`institutional/institutional.css` is **superseded but not deleted** — the five institutional kits
still load it and still render. It declares its own `:root`, and four steps of its spacing scale
collide with the system's under the same `--af-` prefix:

    --af-s5: 24px   vs   --af-space-5: 20px
    --af-s6: 32px   vs   --af-space-6: 24px
    --af-s7: 48px   vs   --af-space-7: 32px
    --af-s8: 72px   vs   --af-space-8: 40px

plus a different type ladder (`--af-type-display` 30→42px against the system's 28→42px). ⛔ **Do not
copy a rule out of that file into a new page.** The failure mode is a page that looks *nearly* right.
⭐ The 7 Aug note that *"adopting the foundation's 24px gutter silently undid my nav fix"* is this
fork stated as a lesson without being named as one. Full table at the head of `chrome.css`.

⚠️ **And the green survives as fallbacks.** `#14594a` is gone as a token value but remains in
`var(--af-accent, #14594a)` fallbacks in `institutional.css` and the five institutional kits — firing
exactly when the token layer fails to load, so the *safety* value is the wrong colour.

## One new question for the owner

Forms now have error states and this palette has **no red**, by decision. The pattern used is
GOV.UK's with the red removed: an error summary at the top linking to each failed field, a 4px
leading rule, and the word "Error:" in the text. It is WCAG-conformant — 1.4.1 forbids colour being
the *only* carrier, so a palette with no red meets it by construction rather than by discipline.
⛔ **The open question is whether a reader can *find* the failed field at a glance without one.**
Adding a measured red is a two-line change; every rule is already written against `--af-state-error`.
