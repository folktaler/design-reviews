Nine organisations

The people working out of this room

Nine organisations use aikyam space. They are here because the room is collaborative and psychologically safe, and because they are all working on social impact in Fort Kochi and Mattancherry.

⛔⛔ The two labelled groups are gone — owner, 10 Aug 2026: “no need to separate aikyam and others. just one list is good.” One flat list of ten.

⚠️ An argument is retired here and it is worth recording rather than quietly dropping. The case for grouping was: excluding aikyam's own five hid a true fact; labelling them keeps the fact and kills the wrong inference — that ten unrelated organisations independently chose this room. He has heard that and decided the inference does not worry him. ⛔ That is his to decide, and it is not a defect to be designed around.

⚠️ Sana Fathima removed on his instruction — nine, not ten. Every count in the copy moved with her. ⛔ A count in prose has to be found and changed by hand every time the list changes, which is the second time today it has needed doing — in the build it is derived from the list and this stops being a task.

⭐ What survives is the part that mattered: the original exclusion was still wrong. The correction was to show all ten; the grouping was the mechanism, not the point. He kept the fix and dropped the scaffolding.

⛔ And the distinction is not preserved by other means. No visual weighting, no “aikyam” doing the labelling's job quietly, and ⭐ the order is the live page's own published sequence — which interleaves them — not outside-then-aikyam. A first attempt at this edit put all five aikyam entries last, which would have been the grouping rebuilt out of ordering. One list means one list.

⭐ run_by_kind is untouched. It distinguishes a person you meet from an organisation behind it — a different axis from ours versus theirs, and this ruling does not reach it. Olimalar is still an organisation here and still the name beside two clubs there.

Working out of aikyam space

  • Community 40

    Works with travellers and locals to preserve the culture and history of Fort Kochi and Mattancherry — bringing people together to observe, record and understand the place and its people.

    “We have been using aikyam Space for multiple purposes, from our internal meetings to full team monthly gatherings. What really stands out is the space itself. It feels like an open home, making it a very comfortable place to spend time and work together.”

    Community 40

  • Thudippu Foundation

    Makes learning dance and other cultural art forms accessible to everyone, through performances and learning spaces for artists.

  • Olimalar Foundation

    Works with children and communities in Fort Kochi and Mattancherry to build confidence, emotional well-being and life skills.

    Runs the Curiosity Club and the Reading Club

    “aikyam space felt like home because they truly let it be. They looked after us like family.”

    Olimalar Foundation

  • aikyam fellows

    Work on urgent needs of the planet and of underserved communities — education, environmental conservation, poverty alleviation, public health, art and culture — and help each other through peer learning, coaching and access to tools.

  • aikyam jobs

    A not-for-profit initiative matching social impact organisations tackling inequality and climate change with the right talent.

  • aikyam school

    A free, open forum for anyone working in the nonprofit sector in India — compliance, finance, HR, PoSH, technology, fundraising, storytelling, team management, or anything else that comes up when you are building an organisation from the ground up.

  • PATTIC

    A support and knowledge platform for NGOs and civil society organisations in India, helping them become more transparent, legally compliant and professionally managed.

  • OASIS

    A collaborative tech-for-good ecosystem helping social impact organisations, NGOs and community initiatives use technology, open-source tools and collaboration to solve real problems.

  • Make A Difference

    Works with children in need of care and protection through education, mentoring and life skills — building long-term support systems with volunteers.

    “aikyam space offers unmatched positivity that creates a conducive environment for open conversations. We didn’t need to plan additional exposure activities for the participants — the space itself provided the inspiration.”

    Make A Difference

⭐⭐ The testimonials are rendered as Airbnb renders a review, and that is a measured decision rather than a stylistic one. Measured on airbnb.co.in today: their dated record has no card at all — background transparent, border 0px, radius 0px — while the photo-led listing card has a 4:3 image and a 20px radius. A card is for a thing you can book; a record of something that happened is text on the page with space around it.

⭐ A resident saying what the room was like IS a review, so it uses the component that already exists rather than an invented quote treatment with rules nobody measured. ⛔ No quotation-mark graphic, no tinted panel, no italic block.

⛔⛔ These four sentences are the strongest writing on the entire site and they sit on a page with 95 visitors in six months. “They looked after us like family” is the proof of everything /about/ merely asserts — and it has never been anywhere a stranger would see it. ⭐ Same shape as the diary: the evidence exists and is filed where nobody arrives.

⭐⭐ Nine organisations, three testimonials — and that ratio is why the owner chose this layout over a logo-led card grid. Thudippu has none, no filler was written, the entry simply ends after its description, and nothing looks missing. ⛔ A card grid would have made the absence visible six times over, because a card has a slot and a slot can be empty. A list has neither.

⚠️ The logos are a small mark in a left rail, not the headline. Nine logos of mixed quality — a WhatsApp screenshot, a webp, a PNG — in identical 4:3 frames would have read as a directory, and pushed the testimonials below the fold under the least interesting thing on the page. ⭐ Words lead because the words are the asset.
